Carte de séjour pluriannuelle (multi-year residence card) — Renewal

The carte de séjour pluriannuelle is a multi-year residence permit issued after an applicant has held a one-year temporary card and continues to meet the conditions tied to their situation (employment, family life, studies, etc.). It removes the need to renew a residence document every single year.

Standard validity length is generally up to 4 years for salaried workers, self-employed workers, and most family-related situations. Some profiles are limited to 2 years, such as certain spouses of French citizens in specific circumstances. Students receive a card valid for the remaining length of their study cycle, with a minimum of 2 years and a maximum of 4 years. Seasonal workers can receive a card valid for up to 3 years under the "travailleur saisonnier" designation.

Renewal applications must be submitted to the applicant's prefecture (or via the online ANEF platform, where applicable) within a defined window before the current card expires — typically at the earliest 4 months and at the latest 2 months before expiry. Missing this window and applying late can result in a late-filing penalty fee (around 180 euros in cases documented outside the ANEF online procedure).

Before renewing, applicants must confirm that the conditions that justified their original card are still met. If circumstances have changed (for example, a change in employment status or family situation), it may be necessary to apply for a card under a different, more appropriate category rather than a straightforward renewal of the same card type.

The renewal procedure is a paid administrative step (a fiscal stamp fee applies, with the exact amount depending on the card category and the applicant's situation). Once the request is processed and the new card has been produced, the prefecture typically notifies the applicant (often by SMS or through the ANEF portal) to book an appointment and collect the physical document.

The carte de séjour pluriannuelle can be withdrawn before expiry in limited, clearly defined cases — notably if the holder's presence is found to represent a threat to public order, or following certain serious criminal convictions specified in the CESEDA (including offenses such as human trafficking, forced labor, or drug trafficking).
