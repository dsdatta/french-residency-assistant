Carte de résident (10-year residency card) — Overview

The carte de résident is a 10-year residency permit for foreign nationals living in France. It can be issued as a first residence document or when renewing a temporary or multi-year card, depending on the applicant's situation.

Eligibility is not automatic after a set number of years in France. It is reserved for defined categories established by the CESEDA (the French code governing entry and residence of foreigners), including: spouses of French citizens, parents of French children, refugees and beneficiaries of international protection, victims of domestic violence or human trafficking who meet specific conditions, and people admitted through family reunification. Applicants who do not fall into one of these defined categories cannot request the card regardless of how many years they have lived in France.

Some categories qualify automatically ("de plein droit") without a minimum residence delay, such as people with a permanent incapacity rate of 20% or higher recognized by a French body or under a bilateral agreement, or holders of a multi-year card for at least 4 years in certain situations.

Regardless of category, all applicants must meet baseline conditions: not representing a serious threat to public order, not being in a polygamous situation in France, having no conviction for violence causing permanent mutilation of a minor, and signing a commitment to respect the principles of the Republic.

Most applicants (except refugees, stateless persons, and people aged 65 or older) must also satisfy a "republican integration" condition: a certified B1 level in French, and a passing result on the civic exam under the "carte de résident" track.

Applications must generally be submitted online, at the earliest 4 months and at the latest 2 months before the current residence document expires. The 10-year carte de résident itself is free of charge — there is no fiscal stamp fee for this specific document, unlike the separate "carte de résident permanent," which does carry a fee (around 350 euros, combining a tax and a stamp duty) when issued at renewal.

Required supporting documents vary by individual situation (family ties, refugee status, length of prior residence, etc.), so applicants are encouraged to use the official online questionnaire on service-public.fr, which adapts the exact document list to their case. Special rules apply to European nationals, Algerian nationals, and certain other specific nationalities, which are handled under separate provisions.

Source: adapted from service-public.fr (Carte de résident de 10 ans d'un étranger en France) and related official guidance, current as of mid-2026.
